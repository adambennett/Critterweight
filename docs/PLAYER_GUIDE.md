# Critterweight — player guide

Welcome to the Critterweight Circuit. This guide covers controls, the fighting basics, every way to play,
and where your data lives. Everything here is also in the game: the **Controls** screen (pause menu or
Settings › Controls) shows your live bindings, and **Tutorial** teaches the basics with Granny Haymaker.

## 1. Controls (defaults; rebind anything in Settings › Controls)

| Action | Xbox-style controller | Keyboard & mouse |
|---|---|---|
| Move | Left stick | W A S D / arrow keys |
| Equipped move 1 | X | J / left mouse |
| Equipped move 2 | Y | K / right mouse |
| Guard (tap right before a hit = parry) | Left trigger | Shift / L |
| Dodge (+ direction; basic move during it = dash strike) | A | Space |
| Grapple / throw / objective interaction | B | F / middle mouse |
| Equipped move 3 / Equipped move 4 | LB / RB | Q / E |
| JUICE (needs full Hype) | Right trigger | R |
| Taunt (fills Hype, risky) | D-pad up | T |
| Switch target | Right stick click | Tab |
| Tag partner (tag-team) | D-pad down | G |
| Nudge camera | Right stick left/right | Z / C |
| Pause | Menu | Esc |

Menus: left stick / D-pad move, A select, B back, LB/RB switch tabs, Y and X are shown on screen when a
screen uses them. The mouse can click everything. Hold/toggle guard, dead zones, sensitivity, inversion,
vibration and the button-prompt style are in Settings › Controls.

Use the **right stick** to scroll a focused text panel or list. The main menu uses eight groups without
a scrollbar. Tutorial is under Fight; AI Lab and Mods are under Create; Controls and Credits are under
Settings. Profile lets you rename Rookie; dialogue uses that name.

**Movesets** is the default combat style. The four move buttons perform the named moves you equip;
their current names, charges, cooldowns and live bindings appear on the HUD. Hold a stream to keep
pouring, steer while it runs, or hold a charged attack and release to fire. All eight fighters have
eight choices and two recommended builds. The **Loadout** button in Versus and the Practice pause
menu let you choose moves. The **Compendium** explains the full pool, types, reactions and mode rules.

**Varga has his own controls.** Four limb buttons, held modifiers and stance changes choose his
martial techniques; his four equipped techniques replace groups of strikes, not four ordinary
button slots. Use his Controls reference and tutorial for the current keyboard/controller bindings.

**Classic** restores Light, Heavy and the two original abilities on those four buttons. Choose
**Rules Editor › Fighters › Combat style › Classic**, save, then select that preset for a match.

## 2. Fighting basics

- **Your body is real.** Hits land where a fist, claw, beak or tail actually touches, and harder when it is
  moving fast. Spacing and timing matter more than button speed.
- **Build around four named moves.** Every ordinary loadout keeps a cheap, cooldown-free basic
  strike string, leaving three slots for streams, projectiles, charges, movement, counters or support.
  A held attack commits stamina and recovery; interrupt it, evade its reach or punish the wind-up.
  Basic strings include their finisher and dodge strike. In **Classic**, Light strings and Heavy
  remain separate, with Heavy during a Light string selecting the finisher.
- **Guard** absorbs most damage but costs stamina; **parry** (guard just before impact) staggers the
  attacker and gives you a free moment. **Grabs beat guard**, strikes beat grabs, guard beats strikes.
- **Dodge** has a short invulnerable moment; press your equipped basic move while dodging for its
  dash strike (Light in Classic).
- **Stamina** (green bar) fuels everything; at low stamina your attacks slow down. **Poise** breaks into a
  knockdown if you keep getting hit. Getting up gives a short invulnerability.
- **Hype** (orange bar) fills as you hit, get hit, parry and taunt; at 100 press **JUICE** for your
  fighter's ultimate state.
- **Elements**: fighters carry Scorch, Brine, Static, Sludge, Grit, Gale, Scrap or Spectral.
  Repeated elemental hits build up a status (shown under the health bar with its name); two statuses
  together trigger a **reaction**. Element zones on the floor (puddles, fires, gusts) affect anyone inside.
- **The cage**: slamming someone into the fence hurts extra (wall splat / cage slam). Some arenas have open
  edges — falling off costs health.

## 3. Ways to play

| Menu | What |
|---|---|
| Fight › Quick Fight / Versus | You against bots (up to 8 fighters, teams, any rules preset and arena) |
| Fight › Practice | A dummy that stands, guards, parries, counters, replays your recording, or fights; hit telemetry |
| Fight › Spectate | Bots only; Y cycles Follow / Locked / Free, LB/RB chooses a fighter, D-pad speed, X hides HUD |
| Fight › Tutorial | Granny Haymaker teaches your equipped moves; Classic and Varga have their own lessons; retry, skip or replay |
| Bonus modes (rules presets) | **Spotlight Hog** (hold the moving light), **Chumball** (knock the ball into the goal), **Absolute Unit** (KO the giant to become the giant), **Chum Rush** (co-op waves), **Tag Team** |
| Campaign | **Career** (one persistent fighter: offers, training, purses, titles; three save slots), **Off the Street** (fresh runs, no permanent power) and **Fight Camp** (persistent upgrades, The Cannery hub), plus **The Bookie's Table** (watch AI fight your build); The Dosage difficulty ladder 1–10; co-op with friends |
| Online | Host a game, join by address or LAN, or connect to a dedicated server with playlists |
| Create › Rules | Make, test-play, save, share and import your own rules presets |
| Create › Arena | Build arenas from props, hazards and spawns; test-play and share them |
| Create › AI Lab | Train your own fighter AI on your PC, evaluate it, then fight or watch it |
| Create › Mods | Turn mods on/off, keep mod sets, import/share packages (data-only, they never run code) |
| Compendium | Fighter models and abilities, elements, arenas, rules and field guide |

Records: custom rules and mods are allowed everywhere single-player; results played with them are marked
"custom" and kept separate from standard records. Online, everyone needs the same gameplay mods as the host.

**Moves grow with your campaign.** Off the Street offers scrolls, move tutors, rest-stop learning
and drills. Fight Camp has training and film study; Career retains learned moves and drills between
bouts. Equip from the moves you know before fighting. Drills trade properties such as focus, spread,
projectile behavior or control, so choose them to suit your build. Co-op choices belong to each
player and are validated by the host. Earlier runs and Careers retain progress and receive a
recommended Movesets loadout; an explicitly saved Classic choice is preserved.

Free camera: LS/WASD moves, RS/right-drag looks, A/Space rises and LT/Shift descends. At the Bookie's Table,
View/Tab switches between flying and its control bar. Watch fights shows actual combat at 1–4x; Skip ahead
hides that same fight and advances it at 8x. You can return to watching immediately.

In the Cannery, the Chum Cannon offers Warm-up, Pressure and Homing hell. Crate Toss rotates a called
target between the three rings: matching it adds 25 points plus a streak bonus, so vary throw strength.
Crates reset when the activity ends. Needles' hub board is a catalog preview; purchases and the listed
services happen at **Shop nodes on the campaign map**, using Scrip. Clout is for permanent hub upgrades.

The Arena Editor's **View** button switches map/panel focus. Palette and Details can be minimized to give
the map more room. When map focus is locked, sticks and editing buttons stay inside the canvas.

Display settings use a resolution dropdown followed by one Keep/Revert confirmation. FSR works on NVIDIA,
AMD and Intel hardware; DLSS is not available in this renderer. Diagnostics › Show FPS adds a counter over
both menus and gameplay.

## 4. Playing online

Main menu › **Online** has three tabs (LB/RB, or Q/E): **Host**, **Join** and **LAN**. Y (keyboard R) runs
the tab's main action. Your player name and the network defaults are in Settings › Network.

**Host a session.** Choose *Matches* (everyone fights under your rules, again and again from the lobby) or
*Co-op party run* (a campaign run played together, started or resumed in the lobby). For matches pick the
rules preset (your own saved presets are marked "(yours)"; practice, tutorial and campaign rules are
offline-only), the arena (your own layouts are sent to the players before each match), an optional
**playlist** (players then vote between a few of its entries), bots with their skill or one of your
**AI Lab** policies, plus max players, password, LAN visibility and the port. **Host session** opens the
lobby.

* Hosting uses **UDP port 7777** (Settings › Network › Host port). Players on your local network can join
  right away. For play over the internet the host's UDP port must be reachable: forward it on the router
  to your PC and allow it in the firewall (there is no relay or matchmaking service).
* **Copy invite** (lobby) copies a line with your address, port and whether a password is needed. It uses
  your local network address; for internet play tell your friends your public address instead. The
  Online screen shows this computer's local addresses.

**Join.** Enter the host's address (an IP address such as 192.168.1.20, or a name; with a keyboard you can
type `address:port`), the port (7777 unless the host changed it), the password if there is one, and
optionally *Join as spectator*, then **Join**. Dedicated servers are joined the same way. Servers you
joined before are listed under **Recent servers** (A joins again, X / Delete forgets one).

**LAN.** Lists sessions announced on your local network: name, rules and arena, players, a lock when a
password is needed. A session you cannot join says why (a different game version, or exactly which mods
differ). LAN discovery must be on for both sides (Settings › Network); it uses UDP ports 7779–7782.

**The lobby.** The list on the left is yours: fighter (opens the character select), team, spectate and
**Ready** (Y / R). The host also sets the rules, arena, playlist voting, bots, bot skill and AI Lab policy,
starts the match (Y / R) and copies the invite. When a vote is open, pick an entry with A. The right side
shows everyone's name card in their equipped profile frame, the cosmetics they wear, their fighter, ready
state and ping, and the active rules. **Rules and online restrictions** explains what is different
online: the host's game (or the server) decides every hit and result, matches never pause and always run
at full game speed, aim assist follows each player's setting unless the server sets it, cosmetics are
looks only, and everyone needs the same game version and gameplay mods. After a match the results show
for a few seconds, then everyone is back in the lobby. B leaves (asks first); when the host closes the
lobby the session ends for everyone.

**Co-op party runs** (host, in the lobby): choose the *Campaign* (Off the Street, or Fight Camp — then
everyone's own Cannery upgrades apply to their own fighter), the *Co-op format* (Simultaneous: everyone
fights at once and revives downed allies by holding Grab next to them; Tag-team: one fighter in at a time)
and the *Difficulty*, then *Deal a new party run* for the 2–4 players who are not spectating (each plays
their lobby fighter) — or pick a suspended party run and *Offer this run*. The members take their seats
(*Take my seat* for a new run, *Rejoin this run* for a suspended one, checked against their own save) and
the host starts it (Y / R); anyone still missing is played by a bot until they come back.

From then on everyone plays the run in the campaign screens. On the map, pressing a stop is your **vote**
(the most votes win, the Captain breaks ties, the timer decides for anyone who does not vote); each stop
shows its votes and the line under the title shows what everyone is doing. Rewards, the shop and rest are
**your own** choices for your own fighter; a personal event is answered by whoever answers first, a group
event is a vote, and everyone sees what happened. Fights are played together: in Simultaneous everyone is in
the cage; in Tag-team the benched partner presses *Switch Target* to tag in, *Taunt* to cheer (Hype for the
fighter who is in) and *Ability 1* for an assist. A fight cannot be paused, but anyone can call a **ref
timeout** from the pause menu (it freezes the fight for everyone; 30 seconds per player per run). Only the
host can suspend the run for everyone or forfeit a fight; anyone else can *Leave the party run* (a bot plays
their fighter until they rejoin). If you lose your connection, join again: you get your fighter back at
once and your seat in the lobby automatically. If the host leaves, the session ends — the run is saved at
its last stop in everyone's party saves and anyone can offer it again later. At the end each player's own
profile is credited once (Clout, records, history).

**When something goes wrong** the game says why and leaves you on the Online screen: nobody answered
(check the address and port, and that the host's port is reachable), wrong password, session full,
different game version or mods (with the differences listed), removed by the host (with their reason), or
the host left.

## 5. Settings highlights

Display (resolution, window mode, monitor, frame limit, VSync, render scale/upscaler, UI scale; risky changes
revert after 15 s unless you keep them), Graphics presets including **Steam Deck**, Audio (master, music,
effects, voice, UI, ambience, mute when unfocused, output device), Accessibility (text size, subtitles and
size, colour-vision palettes, screen shake, flashing reduction, motion reduction, gore Full/Reduced/Off, game
speed and assists), Gameplay (difficulty, announcer level), Network, Diagnostics (FPS/net overlays, logs).

## 6. Your data

Everything you create is stored in your user folder (Windows `%APPDATA%\Critterweight`, Linux
`~/.local/share/Critterweight`): `profiles/` (profiles, settings, recent servers), `presets/`, `arenas/`, `runs/`
(suspended campaign runs), `mods/`, `policies/` (AI Lab), `logs/`. Saves are written atomically with a
backup and a checksum; a damaged file is set aside (never deleted) and the newest good copy is used.
Start with `-- --safe-mode` if a mod setup ever gets in your way.


## New fighters in the v0.9.15 preview

**Varga (Scrap)** imports Jeremy's Cage Rules human fighter and standing moves. He has a special four-limb layout: keyboard U/I/J/K, controller X/Y/A/B. Hold Q/E/R or LB/RB/LT to select hooks/body kicks, straights/head kicks, or overhands/low kicks. Unmodified buttons give uppercuts, a lead teep and rear knee. Guard is L/RT; grapple is Space/left-stick click; slip is Shift/right-stick click. X/D-left switches stance, O/D-down checks, F1/D-up and F2/D-right fire abilities, and releasing V/View fires JUICE. View chords supply target/tag/taunt without spending JUICE. In Cannery sparring, View+RT (keyboard V+L) returns to the hub. His fighter sheet contains the full controls and current binding glyphs. See `kits/f_varga.md` in the source repository for the complete guide. Grappling uses this game's clinch and throws, rather than Cage Rules' ground/submission system.

**Knell (Spectral)** is a haunted diving suit with the standard layout: Ghost Light is a homing projectile, Through the Veil is a phasing escape with an emergence pulse, and Last Bell sends three spectral pulses while protecting the diver. See `kits/f_knell.md` in the source repository. Both fighters have three campaign build paths and appear in normal fighter selectors, the Compendium and Random selection.

## New in v0.9.16: Career, 10-point scoring and the referee

**Career** (Campaign › Career) builds one fighter over many fights, recreated from Cage Rules' career.
Pick a fighter, a ring name (and an optional nickname) and a difficulty (Easy, Normal, Hard: how good
the opponents' corners are). Each week you train one stat — Stamina, Chin, Speed, Power, Clinch or
Defence; the card shows this week's exact gain — or rest to heal faster. Two or three promoters send
offers each week: accept one, keep training through the camp, then press FIGHT on fight week. Offers
show the opponent's fighter, record, rating and a danger tag (FAVOURED, EVEN, UNDERDOG, LONG SHOT),
the purse and win bonus, the weeks to the fight and the rounds. Wins — especially early stoppages —
raise popularity, which opens bigger promotions; each tier caps how famous it can make you, so move up.
At popularity 92 the Grand Circuit offers a five-round title fight; champions defend the belt. Purses
buy gym upgrades (a room per stat, a recovery tank and a head coach). Injuries from a bout (head, body,
legs) heal week by week; until then they slow training and carry into the next fight. The History tab
keeps every bout and a news log. Bouts use Cage Rules: 10-point judges, the referee, three rounds (five
for titles) and possible draws. Everything saves after each action, in three slots per profile; Retire
moves a save aside instead of deleting it. Quitting a bout from the pause menu pulls you out of it (no
purse, popularity −4); once the judges or the referee have decided it, leaving keeps that result.

**Career moves (v0.9.18).** Your fighter starts its career knowing the moves of both recommended
loadouts, with the first one equipped. Two more training choices sit next to Rest: *Learn a move*
spends the week learning one of the moves you don't know yet, and *Drill a move* spends it drilling one
of your moves into one of its two styles (a drill is for good). Neither raises a stat; injuries heal as
in any training week. *Your moves* on the camp page (right under FIGHT on fight week) lets you equip
any of the moves you know before a bout; the choice is saved with the career. Careers from earlier
versions keep everything and get the starting moves.

**10-point scoring** (Rules Editor › Rounds & winning › Scoring, Duel only): nobody can be knocked out
by damage, so the HP bars disappear. Every round is fought and three judges score it — Even card
(everything counts equally), Volume (leans toward clean strikes landed and parries) and Impact (leans
toward damage and knockdowns): 10-9 to the better fighter, 10-10 when too close to call, 10-8 for a
dominant round. Under the timer the HUD shows the round and each judge's running total, and a small
scorecard appears between rounds. After the last round the judges decide (Unanimous, Majority or Split
Decision, or a Draw); a drawn decision goes to sudden death when Sudden death is on. The results screen
shows every judge's card and what the judges counted.

**Referee** (Rules Editor › Referee, Duel only, works with either scoring): nobody can hit a fighter who
is down. When a strike knocks you down the referee counts: get up any time, or stay down to recover
stamina faster until he waves you up after four seconds. Five knockdowns in one round and he stops it — a
T.K.O. win with 10-point scoring, the round with Health scoring. Throws and hazards never count. Jeremy's
Cage Rules referee walks the cage, holds the attacker back ("Back up!"), waves off stoppages and raises the
winner's arm.

The built-in **Cage Rules** preset is the Standard Duel with 10-point scoring and the referee on.

## New in v0.9.17: commentary, new looks and ability effects

**Commentary** follows the fight you are watching: knockouts, referee stoppages, judges' decisions
and round results each have their own calls, every fighter's abilities are called by name (and
whether they landed), and quiet stretches get observations about pressure, momentum, the clock or a
hurt fighter before any joke. The booth remembers what you have heard across rematches, Career bouts
and restarts and avoids repeating itself. The announcer, subtitle and voice settings work as before;
all speech is offline.

**New looks.** All eight fighters were rebuilt with new forms, faces and details. Eyes follow the
opponent and blink, brows and jaws react, and crests, fins, chains, tentacles, hoses and hair swing
with the motion. Every fighter's hitboxes and moves are exactly as before.

**Legacy Varga skin** (Settings › Graphics): shows Varga's original look from before v0.9.17
everywhere you see him. It is your own preference (other players see their own choice) and does not
change how he fights.

**Ability effects.** Abilities, JUICE, charged heavies, projectiles, ground effects, elemental hits and
reactions have new effects in each fighter's style. Every area shows its real shape and reach on the
floor. Settings › Graphics › Effects and Settings › Accessibility › Reduce flashing apply to all of
them.
