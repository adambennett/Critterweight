# Decisions

Consequential engineering/design decisions with reasons. Newest at the bottom. IDs are stable.

## D-001 Engine: Godot 4.5.1 (stable), typed GDScript, Forward+ renderer
Compared (2026-10-14): Unreal 5, Unity 6, Godot 4, custom Rust (Bevy + Rapier), Electron/three.js.
- **Godot 4.5.1** is already installed (matching the owner's other projects), MIT licensed (no royalties/seat
  fees/runtime fees), exports Windows x64 and Linux x86_64 **from Windows** with official prebuilt templates
  (a real Linux binary without cross-compiling), runs headless for dedicated servers and AI training, imports
  glTF natively and at runtime (mods), ships Jolt physics, HDR pipeline with tonemapping, SSAO, soft shadows,
  and has text-based scenes/scripts that agents can author and review.
- Unreal/Unity: require account sign-in/licence acceptance and large installs (C: has ~4 GB free), editor-centric
  asset workflows, heavier Linux/server pipelines; Unity has runtime-fee/licensing history. Not chosen.
- Custom Rust: installed toolchain is 1.76 (too old for current Bevy), Linux cross-builds need a sysroot, and UI,
  audio mixing, import and editor tooling would all be rebuilt. Higher delivery risk. Not chosen.
- Electron/three.js: explicitly discouraged by the brief.
- GDScript over C#: no .NET runtime in exports, simpler modding story, measured performance is adequate
  (the frame and server benches in docs/VERIFICATION.md). Hot paths stay in engine-native code (Jolt, rendering).
- Mobile/macOS feasibility: Godot exports to macOS/Android/iOS exist; nothing in our architecture blocks them,
  but they are not built or claimed for this delivery.

## D-002 Physics: Jolt (built into Godot 4.4+) at 120 Hz
120 Hz gives stable active ragdolls with implicit joint springs at half the cost of Cage Rules' 240 Hz; profiled
in probes (`--probe stand/jab/spar`). Headless multi-world stepping measured at ~17x real time for 4 worlds of
13 jointed bodies on the i7-7700K (feasibility probe, 2026-10-14).

## D-003 Combat model: hybrid active ragdoll
- Every fighter is a ragdoll of 6–16 rigid segments joined by `Generic6DOFJoint3D`. Each joint's angular spring
  (Jolt, implicit, unconditionally stable) chases a target relative rotation computed each tick.
- Targets come from: base poses (stance/guard), **move keyframes authored as effector paths** (fist/foot/horn/
  tail-tip positions in fighter space) solved by two-bone IK, joint rotations for spines/necks/tails, procedural
  gaits (planted feet, step groups) and look-at.
- The root segment hovers on a spring at stance height, is held upright/turned by an angular spring to a static
  anchor, and is driven toward the desired velocity; legs are physical but do not bear weight (arcade control,
  any anatomy).
- Damage is decided by the server's **contact validation**: a weapon sphere on a moving segment must reach a hurt
  shape during the move's hit window with closing speed ≥ vmin along the contact normal; damage scales with
  speed/cleanliness, body part, guard/parry, counters, combo scaling, elements. Physics resolves the bodies
  colliding; the game adds knockback/stagger impulses and muscle-tone drops for readable reactions.
- Cosmetic physics (debris, gore pieces, particles) never feeds back into the simulation.

## D-004 to D-008 (not used)
These numbers were reserved during the bootstrap and never written. Their measurements live in docs/DESIGN.md
(targets and budgets) and docs/VERIFICATION.md.

## D-009 Joint drive convention (measured)
Godot 4.5.1 + Jolt: a Generic6DOFJoint3D angular spring settles where the relative rotation (B in A's joint frame)
equals `inverse(Euler_XYZ(equilibrium_x, y, z))`; `angular_motor_*/force_limit` does not cap spring torque, so
"strength" is expressed through stiffness/damping and stagger (tone) instead. Probe: scratchpad experiment logged in
docs/VERIFICATION.md §Physics.

## D-010 Explicit PD torques rejected
First rig used explicit PD torques with scalar subtree inertia; it went unstable on thin limbs' twist axes (inertia
~60x lower than the scalar estimate). Replaced by Jolt implicit springs (D-009). Standing probe after the change:
uprightness 0.996–1.000, joint tracking errors 0–6°.

## D-011 Title and terminology
Working title **CRITTERWEIGHT** (no existing game found under that name in a 2026-10-14 search; full trademark
clearance is a deferred commercial task). All terminology is original (docs/design/GAME_PITCH.md).

## D-012 Art pipeline
Original procedural art: creature meshes are generated by our own forge tool (tools/forge, Node.js) from
editable creature art definitions (SDF primitives bound to body segments) and exported as **glTF 2.0 (.glb)** with
a skeleton whose bones match body segments; Godot imports them (validated import path). Mods may ship their own
.glb (loaded at runtime through GLTFDocument — data only). Stylized materials (toon-ramped PBR, rim, outline) are
shared Godot shaders. Fonts: Barlow / Barlow Condensed (SIL OFL 1.1, reused from Cage Rules).

## D-013 Audio pipeline
All music, SFX and the announcer "babble" voice are synthesized by our own tools (tools/audio, Node.js) from
editable score/patch files into WAV, imported by Godot (QOA compression). No third-party samples. Spoken lines are
delivered as subtitles + synthesized babble (original, no TTS voice licensing questions).

## D-014 Networking model
Server-authoritative simulation at 120 Hz; clients send input (world-space move + held bits + press counters,
redundantly) at 60 Hz; server sends snapshots (quantized segment transforms, vitals, entities, events) at 30 Hz;
clients render with an adaptive interpolation buffer. No client-side physics prediction (active ragdolls are not
reconcilable); local feedback is cosmetic. Transport: ENet (Godot `ENetMultiplayerPeer`) with our own message
protocol and version/content handshake. Details: docs/NETWORKING.md.

## D-015 Persistence
User data under the OS user-data folder (`%APPDATA%\Critterweight`, `~/.local/share/Critterweight`), never the
install folder. Versioned JSON envelopes with a SHA-256 checksum of the canonical data, atomic write (temp → flush → rotate .bak → rename), recovery
from .bak/.tmp, explicit migrations, non-destructive failure. A file written by a newer format is never written
(Saves refuses; settings, profiles and mod sets go read-only and tell the player); a damaged main file is
quarantined rather than rotated, so the last valid .bak survives; recovery orders copies by a per-file write
sequence number, never by the wall clock.

## D-016 Mods are data-only
Mod packages contain JSON, images, sounds and glTF models only; any script, binary, scene, engine resource
or resource pack is rejected and archives are never mounted into `res://`. Behaviour comes from the shared hooks
DSL and rule options, which already express kits, statuses, techniques and modes. Reasons: a cage fighter with
online play cannot let a downloaded package run code on players' machines or servers; the data surface is
large enough for fighters, rules/modes, arenas, campaign content and presentation; validation can be complete.
Archives are inspected from the zip central directory before anything is inflated (zip bombs, traversal,
encryption, ZIP64 refused) and extracted to a hash-keyed cache. Overrides of core content need consent
(`replaces_core`, D-027). Folder packages are walked without following links, and only validated files are ever
loaded, copied or exported. Details: docs/MODDING.md.

## D-017 Creature inheritance ("extends")
Creature entries may extend another creature and state only differences (objects merge, arrays/scalars
replace, `null` deletes). Used by mods, fighter variants and campaign build patches; the merged result is
schema-validated. Chosen over copy-paste variants so balance changes to a base fighter flow into variants.

## D-018 User rules presets are runtime content
Player-made rules presets are stored as versioned, checksummed saves under `presets/rules` and registered into
the content registry as `user:<id>` after every reload, so every menu, host screen and playlist sees them with
core and mod presets. They are excluded from the multiplayer content hash: hosts send the full rules
dictionary. One table (`RulesValidator.FIELDS`) drives the editor rows, help text, normalization and validation.

## D-019 Tutorial is a mode with data-driven exercises
The tutorial is a `MatchMode` (real combat rules, real fighters) whose ten exercises, lines and success checks
are content data; prompts are filled from live bindings and the fighter's own move names. Each exercise is
proven completable by an automated scripted player. The sparring partner is a scripted input source, so the
same code drives the Kid in the tutorial and in hub sparring.

## D-020 Spectator auto-director in the view layer
Spectating uses the normal fight camera driven by a director that only reads sim events (no gameplay effect):
interest scores, cuts to big moments, wide shots, viewer lock. Playback speed uses `Engine.time_scale` offline
only and is reset when the match scene exits.

## D-021 Platform services: local backend first
All store-like services (identity, invites, presence, achievements, workshop, cloud saves) go through the
`Platform` autoload with a complete local implementation. Steam integration is deferred and documented in
docs/PLATFORM.md; nothing claims it.

## D-022 Cage collision matches the visible fence
Collision walls are built on the same polygon as the rendered fence (side normals at `TAU*i/sides`) and rise
5 m above it; a containment net in `MatchSim` pulls any rig that still escapes back inside and logs it. Found
from mode captures where fighters stood outside the cage.

## D-023 A ring-out is a short absence, not a defeat
A ring-out parks the fighter (inactive) until it returns at the edge. Round-over checks in every mode ask
`MatchMode.is_beaten(f)` (out and *not* waiting to come back) instead of `is_out()`. Found when a campaign run
on The Rooftop was lost with the player's only fighter mid-return: an enemy KO at that moment read the party
as wiped out. Boss "defeated", Chum Rush survivors, tag-team auto-tags and campaign results use it too.

## D-024 Bots respect open edges
On arenas with ring-out edges (and ring-outs on), `BotBrain` cancels the outward part of its movement within
1.7 m of an open edge and adds an inward pull scaled by the tier's spacing skill; dodges use the corrected
direction. A bot that is carrying someone still walks them out to throw. Without it, headliner bots walked
off The Rooftop three times in one fight (8% max HP each).

## D-025 Whole numbers are saved as integer text
`Saves.stabilize` normalizes whole-number floats (up to 2^53) to ints before its JSON round trip. Godot reads
"N" exactly but misreads a large "N.0" by one, which silently changed 53-bit campaign seeds on save and made a
resumed run diverge (35 of 300 random seeds before the fix).

## D-026 Headless simulations start in phase with physics
`HeadlessFightRunner` and the test helper `step_sim` wait for a physics frame before the first simulation step
when they are called from idle processing. The physics world steps on the engine's physics frames while the
match logic steps in our loop, so starting from idle ran one extra logic step before the first physics step and
the same seeded fight could end differently depending on the caller (seen as an order-dependent campaign test).

## D-027 Mod overrides replace core entries only, with declared and consented `replaces_core`
A mod entry may replace (`"override": true`, bare id) only an existing **core** entry, and only when the package
declares `replaces_core` and the player allowed it for that mod version; root `tuning.json`/`elements.json` count
as core overrides too. A mod never replaces another mod's entries or the player's `user:` entries, and when two
mods replace the same core entry the first in load order keeps it and the other is reported. `Content` enforces
this at load from the manifest and the consent list `Mods.apply()` passes in, so it holds whoever reloads
content. Found in review: `override` alone skipped the consent prompt (a mod without `replaces_core` silently
replaced core rules or took over another mod's entries). Building on another mod = `requires` + own entries or
`extends`; cross-mod replacement would need its own explicit capability and consent design first.

## D-028 Bot tiers: one answer per seen attack, reads as a skill, reaction-spaced ladder
`BotBrain` answers each attack it sees begin once (block, timed parry tap, dodge, counter-poke, armored
blow-through, or nothing) instead of re-rolling a defence every think; it punishes openings it has seen (blocked,
dodged or whiffed moves that cannot be cancelled, a parried attacker) with the strongest own move that fits;
`read` is a skill (spotting button-mashing and charge-ups, timing parry taps on an attacker's pattern, not trading
with a read masher in neutral); reach is judged to the target's body, and during the fight the bot learns how its
own moves connect and when an attack reaches it. Perception stays reaction-delayed, nothing is read from the
opponent's input, and every action is a `FighterInput`. Tier reactions are spaced evenly across the jab-blocking
threshold (0.50 / 0.36 / 0.25 / 0.15 / 0.09 s) because reaction time is the strongest skill lever between two
bots (measured); campaign tiers name the same profiles (their stale overrides were dropped). After a blocked
Light the bot uses what the blocked move's animation tells (when the attacker can strike again) to hit back in
a gap or tap a parry for the string's next hit. Found: a
button-masher beat the Headliner 5 of 6, the tier bench was flat (Headliner 4-8 vs Contender, Hall-of-Famer 7-5 vs
Punching Bag), and the old timed-parry branch raised the guard before its press, so it never parried. Tables and
behaviour: docs/BOTS.md.

## D-029 Printed lines are flushed in release builds too

`application/run/flush_stdout_on_print` is on for every build, not only debug ones. A dedicated server's
console log must reach a service manager (systemd, a Windows service wrapper) and its `--log-file` line by line,
and must survive a crash or kill. Found when the packaged tests read a running server's log: release builds
buffered it in 4 KB blocks, so its `ready:` line was invisible for minutes. The game prints only log lines
(no per-frame output), so flushing each one costs nothing measurable.

## D-030 The dedicated server runs one worker thread

The server export (`dedicated_server` feature) sets `threading/worker_pool/max_threads` to 1. Measured on
the packaged 8-fighter server (`test_net_measure`, quiet i7-7700K):
- all 8 logical cores: 45.6 % of one core;
- 2 threads: 40.4 %;
- 1 thread: 36.4 %.

The pool's threads mostly woke for small Jolt jobs and cost more than they saved. An idle server is about
6 % either way. The server is authoritative, so its physics thread count changes nothing that a client or a
replay sees. The game itself (rendering, listen hosting, AI Lab workers) keeps the full pool.
`test_net_measure` measures the server package itself whenever it runs inside a packaged game.

## D-031 The dedicated server paces its own frames

The server no longer uses `Engine.max_fps` to run one frame per tick. It sets `max_fps` to 0 and the
low-processor sleep to 0, and sleeps until the next frame is due with `OS.delay_usec`, keeping an absolute
schedule. On Windows, Godot 4.5.1's frame limiter busy-waits through part of every frame: an empty project at
120 or 60 fps used 4.5 % of a core, all in user time on the main thread. The same loop paced by a plain sleep
used 0.0 %, with the same 120 frames and 120 physics ticks per second. An idle dedicated server dropped from
8.3 % to 0.1 %. Physics steps still follow real time, so a stall is caught up exactly as before.

## D-032 A burst at the attacker's own body is guarded by facing him

An instant area whose centre lies within 1.6 m of the attacker's root comes from the attacker for guarding:
`EffectsSystem.area` sets `guard_from` to his position, and `Combat.deliver` checks the guard against it.
Examples are a stomp at his hoof or a burst around his fist. Knockback still pushes away from the area's
centre, and thrown or placed areas and zones keep their centre. Found in the Dozer mirror: at point blank the
hoof stomp's centre lay beside or behind the defender, so a front guard never blocked it. That cost human
players too, not only bots.

## D-033 Top bots learn what a masher's stream allows

These are reaction-limited habits a good player has, still with no input reading. Full details are in
`docs/BOTS.md` "Measurements".
- **Gap counters adapt per target.** An interrupted one asks for a wider gap next time; three net losses stop
  them.
- **Own ranges are learned across neighbouring distance bands.**
- **A walking attacker is extrapolated over the bot's reaction delay and his wind-up when judging reach.**
- **The guard bridges a read stream's gaps.** A read button-masher is blocked pre-emptively.

Reads were raised (Headliner 0.9, Contender 0.6, Journeyman 0.5), and the Journeyman got block 0.45 and
mistakes 15 %. Outcomes against a pure masher on the acceptance seeds:
- Headliner: 17 / 24;
- Hall-of-Famer: 20 / 24;
- Punching Bag: loses 23 / 24 (stays forgiving).

The ladder stays ordered.


## D-034 Slurry's Head Butt starts like the other jabs

Slurry's L1 hit window opened at 0.09 s with a 3 m/s lunge reaching a 1.7 m root gap. That made it faster
and longer than every other jab (0.14-0.16 s): bots and players lost every jab exchange from just outside
their own range (37 vs 7 jab hits against Dredge in 4 bouts). It now opens at 0.13 s with a 2.4 m/s lunge.
Round robin at 12 bouts:
- Dozer vs Slurry 6-6 (was 3-9);
- Slurry vs Stoke 7-5 (was 9-3);
- Dredge vs Slurry still 0-12, a separate cause in Dredge's play, still open.

## D-035 Hits are felt, and walls never hide the fight

The owner found fights clunky (2026-10-17): mashing seemed to do nothing. A masher against the regular bot
(2 x 45 s per fighter) was within 2 m 73-81 % of the time, but only 15-41 % of its attacks landed, and a
landed hit averaged 2.5-5.3 damage with feedback too small to see: about 2 cm of camera shake, a health
trail that drained at once and no hit pause. Walls and scenery could also stand between the camera and
the fighters. All changes are view-side (HitFeel, CameraOccluders):
- offline matches pause for 50-120 ms on a hit given or taken by a local player (longer on counters) and
  0.2 s on every KO; never online, and the speed returns to what it was;
- a local player's hits zoom-punch and clearly shake the camera (scaled by the camera-shake setting), with
  bigger sparks, a stronger flash, a jolt of the struck body and stronger rumble;
- a hit's chunk stays lit on the health bar for 0.6 s before it drains;
- meshes between the camera and the fighters fade to 78 % transparency, and crowd figures in the line of
  sight or right under the lens shrink away.
The simulation, balance and bots are unchanged. The miss rate stays as it is: attack magnetism for players
would be a separate, measured change (the owner's call, 2026-10-17). Tests: test_combat_feel.

## D-044 10-point must scoring: a judged, knockout-free standard duel (v0.9.16)

The owner asked for an optional Rules Editor setting that replaces the HP-based decision with 10-point
must scoring, with effectively unlimited HP, deterministic judging, visible round scores and a clear
final decision (2026-10-19). Rules gain `scoring` (`"hp"` default | `"ten_point"`) for the standard
mode; anything without the key, including every old preset and save, is HP scoring.

- **Where it runs.** `RoundJudge` (src/sim/modes/round_judge.gd) is owned by `MatchMode` and exists
  only for the standard mode, a two-fighter two-team duel and a round timer (`_setup_officials`);
  otherwise the match runs plain HP rules and logs once. Every other mode is untouched. Presentation
  keys off what the sim produced (`result.scoring`, `hud_state.cards`), never the raw rules flags,
  because local play does not validate rules.
- **Unlimited HP.** Health is held at full every tick, `on_depleted` refills instead of eliminating,
  and `Fighter.receive_hit` sets full health and falls through to the poise/knockdown/stun code so a
  heavy blow's reaction is never lost. Max HP is never inflated (snapshots carry u16 health × 10).
  Damage is still accounted and judged; HP-conditioned hooks and low-health lines no longer trigger.
- **Every round is fought.** No best-of: at each bell the judges score the round (`round_scored`),
  and after the last round the decision is computed (`judges_decision`) before `end_round`, so the
  match winner is set first and only the match winner celebrates. One closing procedure
  (`MatchMode._ten_point_close`) handles the bell and a referee stoppage.
- **What is judged** (Critterweight actions only; no grappling, ground control or submissions): a
  per-team tally from the event stream between round start and the bell — damage normalised by the
  target's max HP, clean melee strikes landed, strike knockdowns caused, throws, ring-outs caused,
  parries and guard breaks. Round score `P = dmg_pts + 0.5·landed + 10·knockdowns + 4·throws +
  6·ringouts + 2·parries + 2·guard_breaks`. Three fixed judge perspectives replace Cage Rules' random
  ±4 bias so judging is RNG-free and explainable: "Even card" (P), "Volume" (+0.5·landed, +1·parries)
  and "Impact" (+0.25·dmg_pts, +2·knockdowns).
- **Cards.** Per judge, |d| < 3 is 10-10; otherwise 10-9, and 10-8 for |d| ≥ 80 or, when the round
  winner scored more knockdowns, |d| ≥ 65 (calibration: D-045). No 10-7, no deductions. Decision
  over totals: Unanimous/Majority/Split Decision or Unanimous/Majority/Split Draw, as in Cage Rules.
- **Ties, consistently.** A level card is a level round. A drawn decision follows the existing
  `sudden_death` rule exactly like a tied HP decision: on → next clean hit wins (method "Sudden Death
  (after a … Draw)"), off → the match is a draw. The Cage Rules preset inherits sudden death on from
  the Standard Duel; the Career turns it off (Cage Rules careers have draws).
- **Data.** Cards, events and `result.scoring` use arrays aligned with `teams` (never dictionaries
  keyed by team), so they survive Saves' JSON round trip and travel as plain data; `hud_state.cards`
  carries the running judge totals for late joiners within the 400 B duel snapshot budget (367 B
  measured during sudden death with a live count). New event types travel as strings, so the network
  protocol version is unchanged; the server alone judges.
- Tests: test_ten_point_scoring, test_rules_presets, test_net_snapshot.

## D-045 Judge, referee and career constants come from measured bot duels

52 headless Cage Rules bot duels (36 same-tier: 12 pairings incl. mirrors × journeyman/contender/
headliner; 16 Hall-of-Famer mismatches), recorded with the referee limit disabled so every limit can
be evaluated exactly offline, and 16 re-runs with the final constants that matched the offline
prediction card for card.

- Same-tier rounds are lopsided: per side 39 dmg_pts and 15 strikes landed per round on average, a
  median judge margin of 25 and 0.77 strike knockdowns per fighter-round. With the initial dominance
  thresholds (30/18) 54% of cards were 10-8, so they were raised to 80/65: same-tier cards are now
  ~8% 10-10, ~88% 10-9, ~5% 10-8; mismatch cards ~3/64/33%.
- Split and majority decisions are rare (~3% of same-tier decisions; the side with more damage won
  27 of 31). Stronger judge perspectives raised them to 12–15% only by also raising 10-8s and draws,
  so they were rejected; human fights may be closer. Recorded as an owner question.
- Career inputs: the fight-of-the-night pace is 44 combined strikes landed per round (85th
  percentile; Cage Rules used 45 for 180 s rounds). Damage taken per fight is ~3.6× max HP after a
  decision win and ~5.8× after a decision loss, so career injuries take 0.07 per % of max HP taken
  (+2 head per knockdown suffered, +10 head when stopped): about 28 after a decision win, 46 after a
  decision loss, 53 after a stoppage loss, 13 after a stoppage win — Cage Rules' scale, where a hard
  fight costs a few weeks of healing.
- Raw data and analysis scripts were kept outside the repository; the method and numbers are here and
  in the RoundJudge/RefereeRules headers.

## D-046 The referee: Cage Rules' striking-only referee as sim rules plus a view-only figure

The owner asked for an optional referee that behaves like Cage Rules' referee, independent of the
scoring setting, and for Jeremy's referee model if it exists (2026-10-19). Rules gain `referee` (bool,
default false, standard mode, duel only).

- **Sim rules** (`RefereeRules`, src/sim/modes/referee_rules.gd), deterministic and RNG-free. In
  every phase a downed fighter is protected (no opponent hit lands while KNOCKDOWN/DOWN/GETUP,
  including fighter-sourced zones; the grab/cage package and environment damage are unchanged) and
  bots back off ~2.4 m while an opponent is down. Only in FIGHT with the round open: strike
  knockdowns are counted once per second (`ref_count`); input gets up at any time, the automatic
  get-up waits 4 s in DOWN (Cage Rules KD_STAY) and is a wave-up (`getup.forced`, `ref_wave_up`);
  staying down regenerates stamina 2.5× (Cage Rules lets a downed fighter clear his head); and
  stoppages are called. One strike-knockdown
  definition (reason poise/move/hit by an enemy) serves judge and referee; throw, hazard and self
  knockdowns are never counted.
- **Stoppage.** Five strike knockdowns of one fighter in one round stop the fight (`ref_stoppage`):
  with 10-point scoring that ends the match as a T.K.O., with HP scoring it ends the round like a KO.
  This is a Critterweight addition — Cage Rules stops fights on accumulated damage, which unlimited
  HP removes. 5 was chosen from D-045's data: 3 would stop 31% of same-tier duels, 4 would stop 25%,
  5 stops ~8% (25% of mismatches), so four or more knockdowns in a round mark a one-sided beating.
- **Ceremony.** A match-ending ROUND_END lasts 6 s when a judge or referee is active, for the
  wave-off/hand raise and the card reveal; all other round ends keep 3 s.
- **Not imported:** ground stand-ups, inactivity warnings and the ground crouch are grappling-only in
  Cage Rules; Critterweight has no ground game (CMB-06).
- **The figure** (`RefereeView`, view-only, no collision, never in camera framing) is Jeremy's referee:
  `tools/cage_rules/import.js` builds it from the retained Cage Rules render.js as `FighterModel(…,
  {ref: true})` (shirt, slacks, shoes, blue nitrile hands) on Varga's rig, with its poses, tuning and
  ceremony times exported to `game/assets/data/cage_referee.json`. It is not a content creature (no
  content-hash change, no gameplay stats). `RefereePose` ports the original pose evaluator (two-bone
  IK, stepping) and reproduces Node-baked transforms to 1e-6 m; the director ports positioning,
  knockdown hold-off ("Back up!"), wave-up, wave-off and the hand raise. Fighters are not forced into
  Cage Rules' line-up (they are physics rigs of many shapes); the referee raises the arm on the
  winner's side beside the winner. It is driven from replicated plain data, so online clients see the
  same referee without extra network data.

## D-047 The built-in Cage Rules preset

`content/core/rules/cage_rules.json` is the Standard Duel (`duel.json`) field for field with only
`scoring: "ten_point"` and `referee: true` added (plus its own id, name and description): three
99-second rounds, sudden death on. The owner asked for exactly that inheritance, so the preset keeps
Critterweight pacing rather than Cage Rules' 3:00 rounds, and drawn cards go to sudden death under the
inherited rule (D-044). It appears automatically wherever built-in presets are listed (versus setup,
online host, lobby, Rules Editor, Compendium) and is validated by the existing preset tests.

## D-048 Career: a persistent fighter career beside the run-based campaign

The owner asked for a conventional fighter career recreated from Cage Rules' career (create a
fighter, fight offers, training and recovery between bouts, records, standing, purses, history,
reliable saves), first in the Campaign menu, without grappling (2026-10-19).

- **Separate from runs.** The Career lives in src/campaign/career/ with its own state (`CareerState`,
  VERSION 1), store and controller; it does not reuse RunState/RunController (a seeded three-act
  roguelike) or RunStore (whose folder listing would show a career as a damaged run). Saves go
  through `Saves` (kind "career") at `careers/<profile>/slot_<n>.json`, three slots per profile,
  autosaved after every action and before a bout. Retire moves every copy (.json/.tmp/.bak) aside so
  backup recovery can never resurrect it.
- **Cage Rules loop kept:** weeks; train one stat or rest; seeded offers from nine original
  promotions in four tiers with Cage Rules' popularity gates, tier caps (42/66/88/100), purses, short
  notice premium, title shot at 92 and one belt; gym upgrades (8 × 5, Cage Rules costs); injuries in
  head/body/legs with Cage Rules healing; Cage Rules' popularity formula; history and a news log.
- **Translations.** The grappling stats BJJ and Wrestling become Clinch (Critterweight's grab layer:
  holds, throws, escaping holds) and Defence (guard, parry, dodge). Each of the six stats maps to
  fixed Critterweight fighter modifiers through one function used for the player and the generated
  opponents (never `defense`, which multiplies damage taken, nor the `poise` multiplier, which would
  count twice). Career power applies only inside career bouts. Bouts use the Cage Rules preset with 3
  rounds (5 for title fights) and sudden death off; finishes are referee T.K.O.s, so the record has
  T.K.O. and decision buckets (the submission bucket is dropped). Carried injuries become fight-start
  handicaps (head → less poise, body → slower stamina, legs → slower movement) because HP is unlimited.
- **Rankings.** Cage Rules has no ranking table: standing is the tier, the popularity label, a
  contender number (#15…#1) in the top promotion and Champion.
- **Fidelity choices:** quitting a career bout from the pause menu is a withdrawal (popularity −4, no
  purse, as in Cage Rules) until the judges or the referee have decided it; after that, leaving books
  the result (Cage Rules settles a fight that has a result). Only a crash re-fights the same bout
  (same seed, opponent and arena).
  Opponents are playable Critterweight fighters with original ring names; no real promotion or
  fighter names. Bot tier follows opponent level (Punching Bag … Headliner) with an Easy/Normal/Hard
  career difficulty; Hall-of-Famers appear only on Hard. Not recreated: Cage Rules' walkable 3D gym
  and heavy bag (the Career uses menu screens) and its look editor (Critterweight fighters have fixed
  art).

  test_career_ui.
