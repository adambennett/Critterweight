# Critterweight 0.9.18

Critterweight now fights with creature movesets. Build a furnace, a tidecaller, a venomous sniper,
a walking quarry or an aura boxer, then bring that build into a real-time cage fight.

## The new combat

- **Eight moves to choose from for every fighter**, with two recommended builds each. Equip four
  named moves and keep your JUICE ultimate. Ordinary fighters still use four direct move buttons;
  hold a stream to keep pouring, steer it with movement, or hold a charged blast and release to fire.
- **Moves with different jobs.** Stoke pours Flamethrower, launches burning coals or rushes in under
  furnace pressure. Dredge sends slow bubbles through space, charges a recoiling Hydro Cannon or
  pulls enemies into a whirlpool. Gusto throws feather volleys, cutting wind and moving tornadoes.
  Dozer raises rock slides and hurls boulders. Slurry spits poison darts, sprays acid and sheds
  ailments. Jitters sets spring pads and chains electricity. Knell channels Night Shade, sends
  spectral lanterns and slips through the veil.
- **Varga keeps his depth.** His limb buttons, held modifiers, stance changes and defensive options
  now select aura palms, comet hooks, shockwave kicks and other martial techniques. Equip one
  technique per group; the input belongs to its group, wherever it appears in the loadout list.
  Connected strikes, dodge counters and checks also open contextual combinations. His in-game
  guide and tutorial explain the inputs.
- **Space and resources matter.** Guard, parry, dodge, interrupt, close distance or force recovery.
  A required basic strike keeps an inexpensive, cooldown-free option available. Types affect
  buildup, status reactions, zones and defensive choices as well as damage.

## Build your fighter through a run

Off the Street adds move scrolls, tutors, rest-stop learning and drills. Fight Camp offers training
and film study alongside its existing progression. Career has persistent learned moves, an equipped
loadout and drill choices. Drills change how a move works: a focused stream, a wider blast, a different
projectile or a control effect. Existing techniques and supplements have been translated; an offer
with no benefit for the current build is excluded where appropriate.

Versus setup has a loadout screen, and Practice lets you change moves from its pause menu. Saved
custom loadouts, campaign choices and co-op state keep the moves you selected. The HUD, Compendium,
controls reference and tutorials follow your equipped moves and bindings. Commentary has new
recorded calls for the named attacks and remains available offline.

## Classic is still here

All built-in game types now start with **Movesets**. To use the old offensive system, open the
**Rules Editor → Fighters → Combat style → Classic**, save the rules and select them for your match.
It is an authoritative match rule shared by the server and clients.

**Legacy Varga skin**, under **Settings → Graphics**, is separate. Either skin works with either
combat style. The v0.9.17 art and commentary improvements carry into both.

## Existing saves and online play

Runs, Careers and profiles from earlier releases migrate while keeping progress. A run or user
preset written before the combat choice existed acquires Movesets; a saved explicit combat choice
is retained. Older campaigns receive their fighter's recommended moves, and later choices are
saved normally. The migration records its source format. AI Lab policies trained for Classic
continue there; Movesets uses the new scripted bots instead of treating old policy actions as
new move decisions.

Everyone in an online game must use **0.9.18**. Judging credits sustained contact without counting
every stream tick as a separate strike, and referees protect downed fighters from the new attacks.
Ball throwing, tagging, reviving and other objective interactions retain their context actions.

## Runtime coverage

The release evidence is maintained in `docs/evidence/verification/v0.9.18/`. Windows scripted
checks cover source, rendered and packaged behavior; network checks use local loopback.
Physical-controller, human listening/playtesting, external-network, Linux runtime and Steam Deck
checks have not been performed by the release agent.
